\section*{Summary -- the Anti-CV Edition}
Software engineer and cloud architect with 19+ years of hands-on experience (12+ of them freelance) -- in other
words, 19+ years of collecting instructive mistakes in Java, PHP, Golang, TypeScript and, lately, Python. A regular
CV lists the wins; this one lists what they cost: the leadership role I was not ready for, the search engine I chose
out of familiarity, the middleware that should not have existed, a microservices architecture that was not open to
change, and trading strategies that died the moment the simulator became realistic. Currently IT architect for
three teams at Union Investment (since April 2026) and running BitterQuant, a private ML research project on
systematic trading. Coding agents now write most of my code under my supervision -- so I review mistakes at a
considerably higher throughput.

\section*{Key Impacts (and the Fine Print)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Scaled wer-kennt-wen for 9+ million members and cut page load times by 60\% -- the company ceased operations
        in 2014 anyway. Fast pages, it turns out, are not a business model.
  \item Union Investment B2B platform: deployments from several days to 30 minutes for urgent changes, 99.9\% uptime,
        and an architecture adopted as group-wide template -- which I proposed, but did not lead.
  \item 100\% production uptime over 3 years for a Golang middleware at Saloodo! -- a middleware I now believe should
        never have been built.
  \item Dependency updates \textasciitilde 9\texttimes{} faster (median 2\,h \textrightarrow{} 13\,min) at Union
        Investment in 2026, mainly by repairing a Renovate setup that had been intermittently broken for months.
  \item Reported backend coverage 34\% \textrightarrow{} 87\% -- by fixing the measurement, which had miscounted
        generated code and tests. My most efficient quality improvement ever.
  \item Skipped a full option analysis for microfrontend integration after a successful PoC and saved
        \textasciitilde 60 person-days -- the rare documented case where not doing the homework paid off.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Recent Roles -- with the Plot Twists Left In}

\subsection*{IT Architect \& Platform Engineer, Union Investment\hfill{\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Apr 2026 -- present}}
\rolecontext{Via percision services GmbH \textbullet{} freelance, part-time, remote \textbullet{} architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in Team Rechner (15+ public financial calculators)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Renovate had been intermittently broken for months before I got it working again; median time-to-merge of
        dependency updates dropped from \textasciitilde 2\,h to 13\,min. Robots, too, need a caretaker.
  \item Cut automatic Playwright retries against deployed sites from 3 to 0 -- the flaky tests stopped hiding. In a
        sibling team's monorepo, 1,065 hidden type errors surfaced (now fixed to zero).
  \item Scheduled API tests had failed silently (orange instead of red), and reported coverage was simply wrong.
        Lesson: before improving a metric, check whether it is lying to you.
  \item Growth moment: operations sits with dedicated teams here; every handover costs communication and mixes
        working styles -- reinforcing my conviction in ``you build it, you run it''.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Cloud Architect, LBBW\hfill{\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Nov 2024 -- Dec 2025}}
\rolecontext{Landesbank Baden-W\"urttemberg \textbullet{} freelance, remote \textbullet{} ``Phoenix'' IT modernization (\textasciitilde 80 people), architectural responsibility for 10 of 30 applications}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Built an R-methodology assessment framework and Solution Design Templates for hundreds of legacy applications
        -- for the Azure-based ``Skywalker'' platform, which stayed production-unused throughout my engagement.
  \item Platform gap analysis as a core deliverable: professionally finding out what a platform cannot do yet. Also
        evaluated Elastisys Welkin as an alternative to keep vendor options open.
  \item Hands-on coding gave way to committees, workshops and Azure Calculator cost estimates. Lesson: architecture
        is still architecture when it is explained to committees instead of compilers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Software Lead \& Cloud Architect, Union Investment\hfill{\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Oct 2021 -- Dec 2024}}
\rolecontext{B2B sales platform InvestmentWelt (80,000 users) \textbullet{} hands-on lead of a team of 5--7 \textbullet{} not the overall lead: architectural responsibility lay with Union Investment}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Proposed the Web-Component-based microfrontend approach that became a group-wide template. Skipping other
        integration paths after the PoC saved \textasciitilde 60 person-days -- not the textbook way.
  \item Cross-system search initially seemed impossible; the answer was plain pub/sub with Azure Event Hubs feeding
        Algolia. Lesson: the boring, proven solution is often exactly right.
  \item 360-degree feedback rated my technical competence 9--10/10; growth areas: slower technical explanations for
        less technical stakeholders, and more openness to critical feedback.
  \item Had to remove team members who did not fit despite integration attempts. The hardest decisions of the project
        had no Terraform module.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Middleware Engineer, Saloodo! (DHL Group)\hfill{\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Dec 2021 -- Nov 2024}}
\rolecontext{Freelance, part-time, remote \textbullet{} sole developer of a Golang middleware syncing \textasciitilde 10 million records to Salesforce}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Took the job alongside Union Investment mainly to use Golang professionally for the first time -- curiosity
        as a career strategy, with a three-year commitment attached.
  \item 100\% production uptime over 3 years. In hindsight architecturally unnecessary: eventing with AWS Lambda in
        Saloodo!'s own AWS setup could have been cheaper, simpler, more robust and faster.
  \item Malformed JSON and incorrect types in the source database forced error tolerance everywhere -- my code was
        robust mostly because its input was not.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Senior Software Engineer, Trusted Shops\hfill{\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Aug 2018 -- Oct 2019}}
\rolecontext{Freelance, Cologne \textbullet{} Subscription team and MARS unit \textbullet{} JavaEE6 modernization, AWS Lambda, Salesforce, Zuora and Twilio integration}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First real microservices architecture: simple changes touched many services, and missing tests and automation
        made maintenance hard. Small services, big changes.
  \item The architecture was not open to change -- a missed opportunity I could not turn around. Since then I am
        skeptical of microservices as overarching architecture and prefer Self-contained Systems.
  \item Introduced Pact contract testing and trained several teams: getting services to agree on contracts was easier
        than getting the architecture to agree on change.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Lessons I Keep Re-Learning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Garbage in, garbage out -- also with the best AI models. Engineering best practices still apply.
  \item The simple, proven solution usually beats the clever one; a realistic simulator beats an optimistic backtest.
  \item Paying down tech debt pays off, and an autonomous, motivated team is gold.
\end{itemize}
