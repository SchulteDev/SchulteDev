\section*{Summary: The Honest Version}
19+ years in software, 12+ of them as a freelancer, consistently learning things the hard way so that
clients ideally do not have to. I started with PHP at one of Germany's largest social networks (closed in
2014), met microservices (now a skeptic) and Kubernetes (even more of a skeptic), and settled on
Self-contained Systems, boring proven solutions and Architecture Decision Records. Since April 2026 I am back
at Union Investment, part-time, as IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in the team
building the public financial calculators -- mostly paying down technical debt so a small team can ship safely
and often. Before that: Cloud Architect for the ``Phoenix'' modernization at LBBW. In parallel I run
BitterQuant, a private ML trading research project in which the simulator has so far been much better at
killing ideas than I am at having them. I work agent-first: coding agents write most of the code; I keep
goals, architecture, review -- and the responsibility.

\section*{Recent Roles: What Worked, What Went Sideways}

\subsection*{IT Architect \& Platform Engineer, Union Investment\hfill\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Apr 2026 -- present}
\roleinfo{via percision services GmbH \textbar\ freelance, part-time, remote \textbar\ IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in Team Rechner (15+ public financial calculators; React, Web Components, Quarkus, Azure)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Revived Renovate after months of intermittent outages: median time-to-merge \textasciitilde2\,h \textrightarrow\ 13\,min; unit tests grew 538 \textrightarrow\ 1,659. Turns out bots also need someone who cares about them.
  \item Cut automatic Playwright retries against deployed sites from 3 to 0, so flaky tests surface instead of hiding. In CI, honesty initially looks a lot like failure.
  \item Request origin attribution: four stories in strict order, because the wrong order would have let a CORS preflight block every backend call. Live with zero changes to the 15+ calculator apps.
  \item \textbf{Lesson:} separate operations teams create friction at every handover. ``You build it, you run it'' remains my conviction, now with fresh evidence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Middleware Engineer, Saloodo! (DHL Group)\hfill\normalfont\small\color{DimGray}Dec 2021 -- Nov 2024}
\roleinfo{freelance, part-time, remote \textbar\ sole engineer of a Golang middleware synchronizing \textasciitilde10 million records to Salesforce}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Took the engagement mainly to use Golang professionally for the first time. Curiosity-driven career planning, rewarded with three years of production Go.
  \item Owned everything: design, code, tests, operations. 70\% coverage on 2,776 lines with 5-second test runs; SELECTs up to 80\% faster via SQL EXPLAIN.

  \item \textbf{Lesson:} 100\% uptime over three years for a component that was architecturally unnecessary. Eventing with AWS Lambda inside Saloodo!'s AWS core would have been cheaper and simpler. A very reliable answer to the wrong question.

\section*{Lessons I Keep Relearning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Paying down technical debt pays off: most of my measured wins in 2026 came from it.
  \item The boring, proven solution is usually right; the exciting one usually needs a sequel.
  \item Quality investment accelerates delivery -- especially when you are the only developer.
  \item An autonomous, motivated team is gold, and team composition requires decisive leadership.
  \item Even the best coding agent cannot fix a vague requirement: garbage in, garbage out.
\end{itemize}
