\section*{Summary}

Software engineer and cloud architect with 19+ years of hands-on experience, 12+ of them freelance.
A regular CV lists what went right; this one lists what went sideways, what I missed and what I
learned from it. I became Head of Development before I knew how to lead, picked the familiar search
engine over the better one, built a middleware with 100\% uptime that in hindsight did not need to
exist, and have been trying to trade the markets with code since 2015. Since April 2026 I am back at
Union Investment as IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, where most of my measurable wins
come from paying down technical debt that the tooling had quietly been hiding. In parallel I run
BitterQuant, a private ML research project on systematic trading on AWS. I work agent-first: coding
agents write most of the code, I keep architecture, scope and quality decisions -- and, conveniently
for this document, the responsibility for whatever goes wrong.

\section*{Key Impacts -- With the Fine Print}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{9+ million users:} scaled wer-kennt-wen with table partitioning and Memcache and cut
        page load times by 60\% -- the network still ceased operations in June 2014. Good engineering
        is necessary, not sufficient.
  \item \textbf{Days \textrightarrow{} 30 minutes:} deployment time for urgent changes on Union
        Investment's B2B portal, with 99.9\% uptime -- on a platform with 80,000 users:
        business-critical, not big.
  \item \textbf{100\% uptime for three years:} my Golang middleware at Saloodo! never went down -- a
        perfect record for a component that, by my own later judgment, was architecturally unnecessary.
  \item \textbf{\textasciitilde 60 person-days saved:} by going straight from a Web Components PoC to
        implementation and \emph{not} running the comprehensive option analysis architects are known for.
  \item \textbf{Renovate PRs \textasciitilde 9\texttimes{} faster} (median \textasciitilde 2\,h
        \textrightarrow{} 13\,min) and unit tests 538 \textrightarrow{} 1,659 in 2026 -- after the
        dependency bot had been intermittently broken for months. Bots need maintenance, too.
  \item \textbf{Coverage 34\% \textrightarrow{} 87\%} for a Quarkus backend -- achieved by fixing the
        measurement (generated code was miscounted), not the code. The cheapest coverage gain of my career.
  \item \textbf{1,065 hidden type errors} uncovered and fixed to zero in a sibling team's monorepo --
        TypeScript knew about them all along, until a type-check gate made its opinion mandatory.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Middleware Engineer, Saloodo! (DHL Group) \hfill {\normalfont\small Dec 2021 -- Nov 2024}}
\meta{Digital freight platform \textbar{} Freelance, part-time, remote, alongside Union Investment}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Took the job mainly for the chance to use Golang professionally for the first time -- a
        motivation more honest than strategic.
  \item Sole engineer syncing \textasciitilde 10 million records to Salesforce: 70\% coverage on 2,776
        lines, 5-second test runs with Dockertest, SELECT times cut by up to 80\%, 100\% uptime.
