\section*{Summary, Honestly}
Software engineer and cloud architect with 19+ years of hands-on experience, 12+ of them as a freelancer, and a respectable collection of mistakes to show for it. Since April 2026 I am back at Union Investment as IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in the team that builds the public financial calculators. Before that: cloud architect in LBBW's ``Phoenix'' IT modernization. On the side I run BitterQuant, a private ML research project on systematic trading on AWS, which so far has taught me more about what does \emph{not} work than about what does. I work agent-first: coding agents write most of the code, while architecture, scope, quality decisions --- and responsibility --- stay with me. My official slogan is ``Crafting solutions that thrive today and tomorrow.'' This document is about the yesterdays: the failures, missed opportunities and growth moments a regular CV politely leaves out. Every number is real; only the tone has been adjusted.

\subsection*{Top Lessons, Condensed}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Technical competence does not automatically translate into leadership; the human factor is the main factor.
  \item Familiar is not the same as right: evaluate, then decide (Sphinx taught me; Algolia confirmed it).
  \item The simplest architecture is often the right one --- and sometimes the best middleware is none at all.
  \item Paying down technical debt pays off, and the whole team profits from it.
  \item Realistic simulation first: the simulator is the most important piece of a trading system.
  \item Even with the best models: garbage in, garbage out. Engineering best practices still apply.
  \item You build it, you run it --- every handover between teams costs communication.
  \item An autonomous, motivated team is gold. Confirmed repeatedly.
\end{itemize}
