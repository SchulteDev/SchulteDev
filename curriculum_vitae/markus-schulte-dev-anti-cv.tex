\section*{Summary}
Software Engineer and Cloud Architect with 19+ years of hands-on experience (12+ as a freelancer), most of
it spent modernizing systems that other people --- and, admittedly, sometimes I --- built. Since April 2026
back at Union Investment as IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in the team that
builds the public financial calculators. Before that, Cloud Architect in LBBW's ``Phoenix'' IT
modernization. On the side I run BitterQuant, a private ML research project on systematic trading, where
the market reviews my hypotheses more harshly than any architecture board ever did. I work agent-first:
coding agents write most of the code, I keep architecture, scope and quality decisions --- and the
responsibility. My official slogan is ``Crafting solutions that thrive today and tomorrow.'' This document
is about yesterday: the failures, wrong turns and growth moments that my regular CV politely leaves out.

\section*{Key Impacts --- Including the Fine Print}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{9M+ members} on wer-kennt-wen.de, one of Germany's largest social networks, on an
        architecture I helped scale. The company ceased operations on 30 June 2014. I like to believe
        these two facts are unrelated.
  \item \textbf{99.9\% uptime} for InvestmentWelt, Union Investment's B2B portal --- for 80,000 users.
        A small audience, but a business-critical one with zero tolerance for excuses.
  \item \textbf{100\% uptime over three years} for a Golang middleware at Saloodo! (DHL Group) --- a
        component I later concluded should never have existed. Flawless execution of a questionable idea.
  \item \textbf{Deployments from days to 30 minutes} for urgent changes at Union Investment, from days
        to hours at toom in 2016. Real progress --- though ``days'' should never have been the baseline.
  \item \textbf{Dependency updates \textasciitilde9\texttimes{} faster} (median 2\,h \textrightarrow{} 13\,min)
        in 2026 --- largely because Renovate had been intermittently broken for months. The biggest
        speedups come from un-breaking things.
  \item \textbf{Backend coverage 34\% \textrightarrow{} 87\%} --- by fixing the measurement, which had
        miscounted generated code, not by writing tests. Honest metrics are underrated, and sometimes flattering.
  \item \textbf{\textasciitilde60 person-days saved} by going straight to Web Components after one PoC instead
        of studying every alternative. I call it ``bias for action''; a stricter architect might call it luck.
  \item \textbf{1,065 hidden type errors} uncovered and fixed to zero in a TypeScript monorepo that had been
        building happily without a type-check gate. Compiling is not the same as being correct.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Recent Roles --- What Went Right, and What I Learned When It Did Not}

\role{IT Architect \& Platform Engineer, Union Investment}{Apr 2026 -- present}
\context{Via percision services \textbullet{} part-time, remote \textbullet{} architect for three funds-squad teams; hands-on in Team Rechner (15+ public financial calculators: React, Web Components, Quarkus, Azure)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Found Renovate intermittently broken for months and revived it: median merge time of dependency PRs
        \textasciitilde2\,h \textrightarrow{} 13\,min. Unit tests 538 \textrightarrow{} 1,659; coverage gates where there were none.
  \item Reported backend coverage jumped 34\% \textrightarrow{} 87\% by fixing the measurement. Playwright retries cut
        3 \textrightarrow{} 0, silently ``orange'' monitors now turn red: CI got more honest, which at first looks like worse.
  \item A pen test flagged a TLS issue we could not fix: Azure Static Web Apps manages its own cipher suites.
        Fix: switch on the managed enterprise-grade edge instead of building our own infrastructure.
  \item Took over an agency-built conversational platform in \textasciitilde4 weeks by reusing our monorepo and
        pipelines; the agency's latest content flow shipped on the deadline day itself.
\end{itemize}
\lesson{Separate operations teams create friction --- every handover costs communication. ``You build it, you run it'' remains my conviction.}

\vfill
\role{Cloud Architect, LBBW (Landesbank Baden-W\"urttemberg)}{Nov 2024 -- Dec 2025}
\context{Freelance, remote \textbullet{} ``Phoenix'' IT modernization (\textasciitilde80 people) \textbullet{} migration to the Azure-based ``Skywalker'' platform \textbullet{} team of 9}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Architectural responsibility for 10 of 30 applications, all headed for a platform that stayed
        production-unused for my entire engagement --- like planning the move into a house still under construction.
  \item Cataloged hundreds of legacy applications (Spring Boot, Tomcat, IIS; Oracle, MS-SQL) via R-methodology with
        reusable templates; cost estimates with the Azure Calculator, the most optimistic stakeholder in any cloud project.
  \item Evaluated Elastisys Welkin as an alternative and identified Skywalker's platform gaps. Daily toolchain: Citrix,
        Webex, Confluence, draw.io --- my diagram-to-deployment ratio has never been higher.
\end{itemize}
\lesson{Architecture without a running platform is a well-documented hypothesis. Finding platform gaps early is the cheapest way to test it.}

\vfill
\role{Software Lead \& Cloud Architect, Union Investment}{Oct 2021 -- Dec 2024}
\context{Freelance, remote \textbullet{} B2B portal InvestmentWelt (80,000 users) \textbullet{} Liferay/FirstSpirit monolith \textrightarrow{} Self-contained Systems \textbullet{} teams of 5--7}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Built the microfrontend toolkit and Azure platform: 1,000+ tests in 10-minute builds, 99.9\% uptime, urgent
        deploys from days to 30 minutes; the pattern became the group-wide modernization template.
  \item Went straight to Web Components after one successful PoC instead of exploring alternatives: \textasciitilde60
        person-days saved. Option analysis is valuable until it costs more than the options.
  \item Unified search across autonomous domains first looked impossible; the boring answer --- pub/sub on Azure Event
        Hubs feeding Algolia --- was exactly right. The complexity was mostly in my head.
  \item 360\textdegree{} feedback: technical competence 9--10/10, communication 3/5 in early rounds, plus requests to slow
        down for less technical stakeholders and to keep opening up to criticism. Printing it here counts as practice.
\end{itemize}
\lesson{Team composition decides success. Removing members who did not fit despite integration attempts was hard --- and necessary.}

\vfill
\role{Middleware Engineer, Saloodo! (DHL Group)}{Dec 2021 -- Nov 2024}
\context{Freelance, part-time, remote, alongside Union Investment \textbullet{} sole engineer \textbullet{} Golang, PostgreSQL, Salesforce, Kubernetes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Took the assignment mainly to use Golang professionally for the first time. Honest motive; the client still got
        a well-tested middleware out of it.
  \item Designed, coded, tested and operated a sync of \textasciitilde10M records to Salesforce: 100\% uptime over three
        years, SELECTs up to 80\% faster after SQL EXPLAIN sessions, the full test suite in 5 seconds.
  \item The source schema had wrong types and malformed JSON, so error tolerance went everywhere. My code was robust
        largely because the data was not.
\end{itemize}
\lesson{The middleware was architecturally unnecessary: with Saloodo! on AWS, eventing with Lambda would have been cheaper, simpler and more robust. Three years of flawless operation of something that should not have existed.}

  \item Led Pact contract-testing training across teams --- effectively treating the symptoms of the architecture I just complained about.

\lesson{Since then I am skeptical of microservices as an overarching architecture and prefer Self-contained Systems.}

\section*{Earlier Experience --- Where Most Lessons Were Paid For}

\role{Head of Development \& Developer, wer-kennt-wen.de}{Aug 2007 -- Jun 2014}
\context{Permanent position \textbullet{} PHP Developer \textrightarrow{} Head of Development (Oct 2008 -- Mar 2010) \textrightarrow{} Backend Developer \textbullet{} social network with 9M+ members, acquired by RTL interactive in 2009, closed on 30 June 2014}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Became Head of Development without sufficient management experience. The company and I both assumed technical
        competence would translate into leadership. It did not, at least not automatically: I underestimated the human
        factor, then learned it through training and reflection and settled on a laissez-faire style that suited me and the team.
  \item Chose Sphinx over ElasticSearch because Sphinx was familiar and ElasticSearch's features seemed exotic.
        ElasticSearch became the industry standard, and the platform later migrated to it --- while I was back as a
        Backend Developer, with a front-row seat. Familiarity is a feature, not a strategy.
  \item Did help it scale: procedural PHP \textrightarrow{} OOP/MVC with Zend Framework, table partitioning, extensive Memcache,
        page load times \textminus60\%, Jenkins CI, and a REST API described in WADL --- a format history has since politely forgotten.
  \item Studied in parallel; my Diplom took until 2012. A 9M-member platform beat lectures as a teacher, but not as a schedule.
\end{itemize}
\lesson{Being good at the work does not make you good at leading the people who do it. And when two tools compete, ask which one will matter in five years.}

\section*{Lessons, Condensed}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Paying down technical debt produces most of the measurable wins --- and the team profits most.
  \item The boring, proven solution usually beats the clever one. Pub/sub was enough.
  \item Familiar is not the same as right: ask which tool will matter in five years.
  \item Before building a component flawlessly, ask whether it should exist at all.
  \item Fix the measurement before celebrating the number --- in coverage as in trading.
  \item Simulate reality before trusting results; paper trading beats beautiful backtests.
  \item An autonomous, motivated team is gold; team composition needs decisive leadership.
  \item Agents multiply output, not judgment: garbage in, garbage out still applies.
\end{itemize}
