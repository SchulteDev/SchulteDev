\section*{Summary}
A normal CV is a highlight reel; this is the outtakes folder, kept in version control. I am a software engineer and cloud architect (19+ years, 12+ as a freelancer) focused on enterprise modernization and cloud-native transformation. Since April 2026 I am back at Union Investment as IT architect for three teams of the funds squad, hands-on in the team behind the public financial calculators. In parallel I run BitterQuant, a private ML research project on systematic trading on AWS. Before that I was Cloud Architect on LBBW's ``Phoenix'' IT modernization. I work agent-first: coding agents write most of the code under my supervision, and I keep the architecture, scope and quality decisions --- and therefore the blame.

\textbf{How to read this:} each role lists what worked, what did not, and what I would do differently. Numbers are real and taken from my career records; opinions are mine and have aged at different speeds.

\section*{Recent Roles --- What Worked, What Didn't, What I Would Change}

\subsection*{IT Architect \& Platform Engineer at Union Investment\when{Apr 2026 -- present}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Architect for three funds-squad teams and hands-on in Team Rechner: 15+ public financial calculators, each shipped as React app and as Web Component, backed by a Quarkus platform.
  \item \textbf{Win:} request-origin attribution via W3C Trace Context and OpenTelemetry baggage, live since October 2026 with zero changes to the calculator apps (stories written by me, built by a colleague). \textbf{Near-miss:} the stories had to run in strict order, otherwise the CORS preflight would have blocked every backend call.
  \item \textbf{Win:} took over a conversational advisory platform from an external agency and had it in production in about 4 weeks as a static build inside the existing monorepo. The best new component is the one you do not add.
  \item \textbf{Unglamorous truth:} most measured results are tech-debt payoffs: Renovate median time-to-merge \textasciitilde2~h \arr{} 13~min, unit tests 538 \arr{} 1,659, automatic Playwright retries 3 \arr{} 0 so flaky tests stop hiding. Users notice nothing --- which is the point, and the pitch problem.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Earlier Roles --- Where My Mistakes Were Cheaper}

\subsection*{PHP Dev, Head of Development, Backend Dev at werkenntwen\when{Aug 2007 -- Jun 2014}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One of Germany's largest social networks (9+ million members). I helped turn procedural code into a layered, object-oriented Zend-based architecture, built a REST API with WADL for mobile, and scaled with table partitioning and Memcache; page load times dropped 60\%.
  \item \textbf{Failure \#1 --- Head of Development (2008--2010):} I took the role without management experience; the company and I both assumed technical competence would translate into leadership. It does not. Training and reflection led me to a laissez-faire style that suited me and the team --- which sounds much better when done on purpose and called ``team autonomy''.
  \item \textbf{Failure \#2 --- Sphinx over ElasticSearch:} I chose Sphinx because I knew it; ElasticSearch seemed exotic. It became the industry standard, and the platform migrated to it when I returned as Backend Developer. I got to migrate my own conservative choice.
  \item Introduced Jenkins CI, PHPUnit coverage analysis, Selenium tests and Xdebug profiling; implemented brute-force, XSS, CSRF and SQL-injection protection. I stayed until the lights went out on 30 June 2014.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Lessons I Keep Relearning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Familiar is not the same as right (Sphinx), and technical skill does not automatically make a leader.
  \item The straightforward solution usually wins: Pub/Sub for search, a static build for the conversational platform.
  \item Quality investments pay back fast, even for a team of one; and tech debt is where most of my measurable wins hide.
  \item Garbage in, garbage out --- the best AI models included. Engineering practices still apply, and the simulator is the most important piece of a trading system.
  \item Every handover costs communication effort, which keeps me a ``you build it, you run it'' believer.
\end{itemize}
